Operational Analysis of a Concrete Batching System

Identifying hydraulic and process inefficiencies at a construction materials plant

Problem

During my internship at Fragilda Construction Company, I observed recurring delays and inconsistencies in the concrete batching process, particularly around the hydraulic mixing and material feed systems.

What I Observed

Pressure drops in the hydraulic lines during peak operation, uneven aggregate distribution in the mix, and downtime caused by manual overrides on automated sequences.

Key Issues

Worn hydraulic seals reducing system pressure by an estimated 15â€“20%, inconsistent calibration of aggregate feeders, and lack of a preventive maintenance schedule for critical components.

Recommendations

Proposed a scheduled maintenance protocol for hydraulic components, recalibration of feeder systems at 2-week intervals, and a simple daily checklist for operators to flag pressure anomalies early.

02

Spatial Distribution of Trace Metals in Soil

Mapping As, Pb, Cd, and Ni contamination patterns using geostatistical methods

The Question

What is the spatial distribution pattern of heavy metal contamination in soils across Abuakwa South Municipal, and where are the hotspots?

The Data

50+ georeferenced soil samples analyzed for Arsenic, Lead, Cadmium, and Nickel concentrations, processed through systematic sampling across the municipality.

Method

Applied Ordinary Kriging and spline interpolation in QGIS and Python to generate continuous surface maps from point data. Used semivariogram analysis to model spatial autocorrelation.

Insight

Identified concentrated contamination clusters near artisanal mining zones and agricultural areas, with Arsenic levels exceeding WHO thresholds in 3 distinct zones. Results support targeted remediation planning rather than blanket interventions.

Analysis

Applied SPT correction methods (energy correction, overburden correction) to raw field data. Cross-referenced soil classifications with local geological maps to validate consistency.
